## The measures

- **Graders:** regex checks per case (kept facts, flagged words, the em-dash cap, no new numbers).
- **Lint delta** (`eval rewrite-delta`, free): lint BLOCK and WARN findings per piece, with and without the plugin.
- **Blind readers:** each with-plugin piece is paired with the same run of the no-plugin arm, cut to the same length,
  and a Claude model is asked which of the two a person wrote, in both orders. The number below is the share of
  answers that picked the plugin's piece: 0.50 means readers can't tell them apart. Wilson 95% intervals in brackets.
  Haiku has a strong first-position bias (it picks A in about 80% of answers), so Opus and Sonnet carry the result.

What it says:

1. **Drafting is where plainspoken is strongest,** and strongest with the nudge and the rule on: both readers pick its
   piece as the person's in roughly four of five pairs. That path is also cheap (2–3 turns: draft, lint on stdin,
   fix).
2. **Rewrites were the weak spot.** Before tuning, a humanize rewrite read no more human than Opus 5.5's own; the
   baseline wins partly by inventing small personal details, which the fabrication guard rightly forbids. Tuning
   round 1 (cut empty claims instead of restating them; write as the sender would) moved Opus from 0.51 to 0.67 on
   plain asks and from 0.44 to 0.64 on the slash arm. Sonnet moved less. Round 2 (contractions, one long sentence,
   first person) didn't help and was reverted.
3. **Lint and readers disagree on rewrites:** the tuned rewrites carry slightly more WARNs (mostly r7d, no sentence
   over 25 words, and rHM, no human markers) because they're shorter, yet read as more human. The lint is a floor,
   not the target.
4. **The graders sit at the ceiling** for a strong baseline (both arms pass nearly every regex), so they can't show a
   difference; the readers can.

## Triggers, smoke and judges

- **Triggers ×3** (42 cases, US$10.23): 0.94 overall. Misses: `write` on a text message 0/3, `write` on a video script
  2/3, `check` on "does this read like AI?" 1/3, `register-check` slash 2/3. Every miss was a one-turn direct answer.
  After the `write` and `check` description changes: **write 11/11, check 3/3, humanize 7/7 cases, all 3 of 3 runs.**
- **Smoke ×2** (12 skills): 23/24 runs; the miss was the setup grader reading a Before/Now table's "off" column (grader
  fixed; re-run 2/2).
- **Judges** on the 232-case golden set (Claude CLI on the API key): Opus ×2, Sonnet and Haiku all meet the 0.8 bar in
  every group. Haiku did it for US$0.14, which makes it a credible cheap judge (still an upper bound: most golden
  cases were labelled by Claude).

## Changes kept

- `humanize`: calibration rule 2 "cut empty claims; don't restate them" and the sender's-voice line in rule 4.
- `write` description names text messages, captions and scripts, short or long; `check` description names scripts
  and "any text, even one line". Always-on budget 478/500.
- Evals: `evals/drafting`, `evals/drafting-hooks` (12 content types), `evals/longform`, `evals/fix`, 6 more rewrite
  cases, 6 write trigger cases; `eval rewrite-delta --save`, marked pieces, errored runs skipped, plugin-install notes
  stripped from pieces.

## Found along the way

- A no-plugin arm given `/plainspoken:humanize …` sometimes says the command isn't installed; that note made the
  baseline look machine-made and flattered the plugin until it was stripped (the first slash-arm reading, 0.57/0.61,
  fell to 0.44/0.51).
- Briefs need calendar-true dates: both arms stopped to point out that "Tuesday 4 November" is a Wednesday in 2026.
- The eval home holds a `.git`, so cases that write reports need their own project marker (the scaffold's `git init`).
- The org has a monthly API spend limit; it, not the credit, stopped the campaign.

## Round 3 (v0.8.0 → v0.9.0, frugal; ~US$18 of a US$30 cap)

| Suite | What it asks | Opus | Sonnet | Lint WARN/piece, with vs without |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| fix ×5 (v0.8.0) | "fix anything that sounds like AI" on a saved stiff draft, 4 drafts | **0.81** [0.65–0.91] | **0.84** [0.68–0.93] | 1.06 vs 0.88 |
| longform, thin briefs | 500–600 words from ~8 facts | 0.17 (1/6) | 0.67 (4/6) | 3.67 vs 2.67 |
| longform, full briefs, v0.9 routing | 450–500 words from 9–12 facts and a quote | 0.50 (3/6) | 0.83 (5/6) | **0.33 vs 2.33** |

- **Fix** routes to `humanize` in file mode plus a lint (5–6 turns, about US$0.10 a case), and its rewrites of stiff
  drafts read as the person's in about four of five pairs.
- **Long-form found two real gaps.** With thin briefs, `write` stopped short of the length rather than invent (the
  right call; the baseline padded with invented colour, which readers rewarded), but it also skipped its own check
  and claims steps. With full briefs and the nudge + rule on, the model drafted by hand and never ran `write` or the
  lint. Fixes in v0.9: `write` always runs steps 6 and 7 before reporting; the nudge and the drafting rule send
  long-form asks (blog post, article, page, newsletter, report, or 250+ words) to `write`. After the fix every long
  piece went through `write` with the full check (48–78 turns, US$1.8–2.9 each) and carried far fewer lint findings.
  plainspoken's long pieces are shorter (290–400 words against 440–470): it won't pad with detail the brief doesn't
  give. Three pairs is too few to read the reader numbers as more than direction.
- **Smoke ×1** on v0.8.0: 12/12.
