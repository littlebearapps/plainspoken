# Answer-first

The reader came with a question. Answer it at the top, then earn the answer.

## Lead with the answer

The first paragraph gives the answer in plain words: the number, the decision, the step. Someone who reads only that
paragraph should leave with the main point.

> Most small cafes can switch card readers in a week. The slow part is the contract you're leaving, not the new
> machine.

## Then why, then how

After the answer, give the reason it holds, then what the reader does about it. Each section follows the same order
at its own scale: its first sentence says what the section shows, and the rest backs it up.

## Headings say what the section says

A heading is a short statement of the section's point, so the headings alone read as a summary.

- Instead of "Contract terms", write "Check your exit fee before you switch".
- Instead of "Timeline", write "Most switches take five working days".

## Close with the next step

End on the one thing the reader does next, and when. Don't restate the article. If there's a real limit (when this
advice stops applying, or when to get a professional), say it plainly just before the close.
