# How strong is the evidence?

When a piece gives recommendations or findings a reader will act on (a report, a review, an audit, advice), tag each
material one with how strong its evidence is. Use one of four plain labels:

| Label | Means | Fits when |
|---|---|---|
| **Strong evidence** | Direct evidence you reviewed supports it; fine to act on now. | You checked it in the data. |
| **Directional evidence** | The direction holds; the size is still to be measured first. | Public signs agree. |
| **Limited evidence** | A plausible lead, not yet enough to act on. | One thin source. |
| **Not assessed in depth** | Not examined closely enough for a firm call. | It was outside this scope. |

Prefer these words to "high, medium or low confidence": readers hear "low confidence" as "bad advice", while
"limited evidence" says what's missing.

## Match the verb to the label

Directional or limited evidence supports verbs like explore, test, check and prepare. Commit verbs (launch, spend,
build, switch) need strong evidence. Never put a commit verb on public evidence alone.

> **Directional evidence:** Test a weekday lunch special for a month before you change the menu. The foot-traffic
> counts point that way, but they don't say how many people would buy it.

## The label isn't a source

A label frames a judgement; it doesn't support one. In the claims ledger a labelled recommendation is usually
`opinion_or_inference`. The evidence under a **Strong evidence** label still needs its own claim, verified or first
hand, like any other figure.

## Recommendations someone else carries out

This applies to reports, audits and advice that will be handed on, where another person or a provider makes the
change. For each material recommendation, name the item affected, the change or check, who usually does it (a role,
never a person you'd have to invent), what confirmation should come back, and what's still unknown. Describe the
change in plain terms and don't invent platform-specific steps, because the person doing it knows their own system.

> **Strong evidence:** The opening hours on the club's map listing don't match its website. Whoever manages the
> listing, often the secretary, changes it to match the site and sends a screenshot once it's live. Still unknown:
> how long the listing has been wrong.
