# Public-copy checklist

Run this once, before a block of public copy ships, and give each item PASS, FAIL or n/a with the line quoted so the
writer can find it. Reading the fail count is house judgement, not a sourced standard: with no fails the block is
ready to ship, one or two fails can be fixed in place, and three or more mean the block needs a rewrite rather than
a polish.

1. **Surface.** Is it clear what kind of copy this is (page, email, post, ad, proof, microcopy), and does it follow that kind's framing? Fails when a post is written like a brochure or a button label like a paragraph.
2. **Reader benefit.** Can the reader's gain be said in plain words they could repeat to a friend? Fails when the opening describes the writer's work and never says what changes for the reader.
3. **Control.** Is each verb something the writer controls, or softened where they only influence? Fails when a line promises a result that depends on the reader or a third party.
4. **Specificity.** Is each claim concrete enough to picture? Fails when a sentence could sit unchanged on a competitor's page.
5. **Distinctness.** Do separate services or sections sound different from each other? Fails when swapping two headings changes nothing.
6. **Method placement.** Is the method there where it builds trust, and below the benefit? Fails when tools, steps or jargon lead the page.
7. **Constraint honesty.** Are minimums, dependencies, exclusions and "not yet" conditions visible somewhere the reader will see them? Fails when a limit is hidden or only appears at the point of paying.
8. **Audience nouns.** Do the people named include everyone the page serves? Fails when a shared page speaks only to one kind of customer.
9. **Voice.** Does it sound like the profile's writer talking to the profile's reader, in the profile's locale? Fails when it reads like a company deck or imports another country's posture.
10. **Action.** Is there one plain, specific next step? Fails when the page ends with several equal options or a vague invitation.
11. **AI tells.** Read aloud, with the checker's output beside you: any of the copy tells a to p? Fails when a tell survives the read-aloud, or the checker reports a BLOCK finding.
