# The voice check

For every section, ask one question:

> Does this sound like the author, or like an AI explaining the author?

## What drift looks like

- Generic, hedged summaries where the author is specific. The author names the part, the date and the number; the
  draft says "various factors".
- Tidy symmetry where the author wanders: matching paragraph lengths, even sentences, a neat closing line.
- The author's opinions softened into "some people say" or "it could be argued".
- Openings and closers the author never uses, or habits from the profile's `habits:` line missing for pages on end.
- Moves the profile lists under `avoid moves:`.
- Rhythm far from the `rhythm:` line: a section with no short sentence, or with nothing longer than twelve words.

## How to compare

Take 2 to 3 anchors of the section's type (opening, explainer, opinion, story, list-intro, closing). Read the anchors
first, then the section. Look at what the author does with the first sentence, how specific the nouns are, how long
the sentences run and where the opinion sits. A section that differs from the anchors on one point is a note. A
section that differs on most of them drifts.

Judge against the anchors and principles, not against your own sense of good writing. If the profile has no anchor of
that type, say so and judge against the principles only.

## Report shape

```
Voice report: <profile name>

## <section heading>
sounds like the author
(or)
drifts
- "<quoted line>" -> <anchor id or principle it drifts from>, and how
```

Quote the draft exactly. Don't offer a rewrite here: Apply does that.
