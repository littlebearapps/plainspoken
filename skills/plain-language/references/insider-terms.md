# Insider terms

An insider term is a word the reader wouldn't use. It depends on the reader, so this file gives a method and no list.
A word that's ordinary to a bookkeeper is noise to a gardener, and the reverse holds too.

## Spotting them

Read the text as the reader named in the profile and stop at any of these:

- **Acronyms and short forms** the reader hasn't met, including ones the writer uses every day.
- **Tool and product names** used as if they explained themselves.
- **In-house names** for a process, a stage or a file, which only the team uses.
- **Words the reader would never say to a friend.** If you'd have to put on a voice to say it aloud, flag it.
- **Nouns stacked on nouns** (a four-word chain of nouns with no verb in it), where the reader has to guess which
  noun does what to which.

## Glossing

1. Say it in plain words first: what it is or what it does for this reader.
2. Put the term itself in brackets after the plain words, only when the reader will meet it again (on a form, in a
   menu, on a phone call). Otherwise leave it out.
3. Gloss once, at the first use. Repeating the gloss every time is its own kind of noise.
4. Keep the gloss as short as the term was. A gloss that needs its own gloss means the sentence should be rebuilt.

## Cutting

Cut the term, and the words around it if needed, when the reader doesn't need it to act. A step the reader never
sees doesn't need its name in the text. If cutting changes what the sentence claims, stop: that is a meaning change,
not a plain-language fix.

## The glossary file

The profile can point at a glossary: a small file of terms already settled for a reader, so every draft glosses them
the same way. One entry per term, three parts:

```
term: direct debit
gloss: money taken from your bank account on a set date, with your OK
for: new customers
```

or as a definition-style block:

```
direct debit
  : money taken from your bank account on a set date, with your OK (for: new customers)
```

Suggest entries after an audit, listing the term, a gloss and who it's for. Add them to the file only when the user
asks you to.
