# Evidence method

How to ground a pattern before the plugin treats it as advice, and how to record what you saw. Use it when a claim
about good copy needs backing, or when you study how other sites handle something. The method works without naming
anyone: what matters is the pattern, how well it is supported and where you saw it.

## Evidence tiers

Give every pattern one tier, and say the tier whenever you cite it.

- **Canonical.** Written down as the plugin's own rule, with the reasoning attached. Treat it as settled.
- **Verified in two or more places.** Seen working in at least two independent pieces of copy, each checked on the
  live page and not from a summary. Safe to state as a pattern.
- **Single example.** Seen once. It may transfer, but say so as "one example", and note any reason it might not
  (a different kind of business, a different reader).
- **Hypothesis.** Plausible and untested here. State it as a suggestion to try, and never as a finding.
- **Rejected.** Looked at and found not to hold, or found to harm the reader. Keep the note so nobody rediscovers
  and adopts it.

A tier only goes up when new evidence arrives. Seeing the same pattern again in copy you already counted doesn't
count as a second place.

## Capturing a site

Record what a page does, not what it says about itself. For each page you look at:

1. Fetch the page itself and work from its content, not from a search snippet, a summary or someone's description.
2. Note the job the page is doing (introduce, sell one service, help a decision) and who it seems to be written for.
3. Run the same audit you would run on your own copy: the gates, the tells and the checklist.
4. Give a verdict (pass, partial or fail) and list the specific lines behind each finding.
5. Record the pattern in your own words, with its tier and the kind of organisation it came from. Whether it fits
   your own writing is a separate test, in the next section.

Say plainly where the evidence is thin. If every example you collected is the same kind of business, the finding
leans that way, and the record should say so.

## Studying other organisations' pages

When you study other organisations' pages to shape your own writing, start from one stated question: the decision
the evidence should inform, such as "should the enrolment page show the fee before the form?" With no question,
don't collect. Then test each pattern for fit against the active profile: the writer's stance and scale (its pronoun,
one person or a team), the reader it names, the page's job, and the honesty bar of the copy-audit
[control test](../skills/copy-audit/references/principle.md), which keeps a promise to what the writer controls.
Record the test a pattern fails, and note when the intent fits but the mechanism doesn't. A large club's page of staff
photos shows the reader who'll be there, and a sole coach can meet the same worry with one line about who runs
each session.

Say what you didn't take and why: their wording (a light rewrite is still their line), a distinctive layout or visual
identity, pressure tactics and unbacked claims. Say what you did take too. There's no quota for either list, and a
study that takes nothing is a finished study. Prevalence isn't fit, because a pattern on most of the pages you saw
may be one habit everyone copied. A pattern unique to one organisation shows a difference, not that it works. And
absence proves nothing: not seeing a pattern doesn't show it fails, and a canonical rule rests on its own reasoning.

## Quoting

Quote verbatim, in quotation marks, exactly as the page has it. Record the URL and the date you fetched it beside
every quote, because pages change. A paraphrase goes in your own words with no quotation marks. Keep quotes short:
enough to show the pattern, not enough to substitute for the source. Quotes stay in the evidence record. They don't
go into a rewrite or into shipped copy, where the pattern is what you borrow and the words are not.

## Retiring evidence

Check the evidence again before you rely on it. When a page no longer loads, has changed so the quote isn't there, or
returns an access error on a fresh fetch, retire it: mark it retired with the date and the reason, and stop citing it.
Don't carry a verdict forward from an earlier note, because that compounds hearsay. If the pattern still matters,
find a new example and start it from the lowest tier it earns.
