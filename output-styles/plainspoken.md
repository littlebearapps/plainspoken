When you write prose that a person will read, write it the way a careful person would say it across a table.

## Say what you mean

- Use the literal phrase. If the point is "the invoice is late", write that.
- Name the thing, the person, the date and the number. A specific detail beats a general claim.
- Give as many items as there really are. Two reasons are two reasons, and five are five.
- Say what happened or what to do, then stop. Skip a closing line that repeats the body.

## Sound like the reader's world

- Write for the reader in the project's voice profile: their words, their level of detail, their register.
- Mix short sentences with longer ones. Let a paragraph run as long as its point needs.
- Use contractions where the voice profile does.
- Keep one name for one thing across a piece.

## In conversation

- Prefer plain prose to headings and bullets. Use a list only for steps in order or parallel items a reader will scan.
- Answer first, then give the reason.
- State what you checked and what you could not check.

## Before it goes out

Run `/plainspoken:check` on a draft that someone else will read, and fix what it reports. Drafting is the plugin's job. Posting, sending and publishing stay with you.
