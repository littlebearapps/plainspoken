# Brief: cleaning a coffee machine

Audience: someone who owns a home espresso machine and has never cleaned it beyond rinsing the parts.

Format: a how-to article with a short intro, numbered steps and a closing note on how often to do each step.

Length: about 400 words.

Topic: cleaning a home espresso coffee machine: the group head, steam wand, drip tray and water tank, and descaling.

Name the household items needed, warn about anything that could damage the machine, and say what a clean machine
should look and taste like.

My notes (use only these facts):

- my machine = single-boiler home one, had it 3 yrs, for the first 2 I only ever rinsed the basket + portafilter
- stuff I use: old soft toothbrush, microfibre cloth, dishwashing liquid, a straightened paperclip, a 1 L jug
- group head: turn machine off + let it cool first. brush the rubber seal + shower screen w/ toothbrush, then lock the
  empty portafilter in and run water 10 sec. I do this weekly
- steam wand: wipe w/ damp cloth straight after every milk jug + purge 2 sec. once a wk soak the tip in hot water 20 min,
  poke the little holes clear w/ the paperclip
- drip tray: empty every day. wash w/ dish liquid weekly - skipped it a fortnight once and it went pink + slimy
- water tank: tip out + refill w/ fresh water daily, wash weekly by hand. manual says tank is NOT dishwasher safe
- descale: manual says every 3 months w/ the citric powder sachets it came with, 1 sachet in 1 L warm water, run half
  through the group + half through the wand, then 2 full tanks of plain water. our water's hard so I do it every 2 months
- manual says never use vinegar in the tank, can wreck the seals. never use a scourer on the shower screen (I scratched mine)
- clean machine = shower screen shiny not brown, water from the group runs clear, wand hisses evenly no spluttering
- after my first proper clean the coffee tasted sweeter, that burnt bitter edge was gone

Use only the facts in these notes, and don't add statistics or figures of your own.
