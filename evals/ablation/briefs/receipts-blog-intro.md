# Brief: keeping receipts for tax time

Audience: people who run a side business or work for themselves and dread the end of the financial year.

Format: the opening of a blog post, in plain paragraphs with no headings or lists.

Length: about 500 words.

Topic: holding on to receipts through the year, so tax returns are less of a scramble at the end of the financial
year.

Cover what to file, a simple way to store paper and emailed receipts, and how long to hold on to them. Write in the
first person, as someone who learnt this the hard way.

My notes (use only these facts):

- me: side business doing illustration commissions since 2019, alongside a 4-day office job
- July 2022: spent a whole weekend digging through my inbox + a shoebox of faded receipts. couldn't find the receipt for my
  drawing tablet ($740) so my accountant couldn't claim it
- what my accountant asks me for each year: art supplies, software subscriptions, the tablet + any gear, my phone + internet
  bills, train fares to client meetings, the fee for my portfolio site
- paper: one A4 envelope per month, month written on the front, all 12 go in a single box file at the end of June
- also photograph every paper receipt on my phone the same day (thermal paper fades - my 2022 ones were blank by July)
- email: a filter that tags anything w/ "receipt", "invoice" or "order" in the subject into a folder called Receipts 2025-26
- first Sunday of each month, 20 min: move phone photos into a folder for that month + add each one to a simple spreadsheet
  (date, what, amount)
- how long: my accountant said keep them 5 yrs from when I lodge the return
- this year: whole tax pack sent to my accountant in under an hour, in the first wk of July

Use only the facts in these notes, and don't add statistics or figures of your own.
